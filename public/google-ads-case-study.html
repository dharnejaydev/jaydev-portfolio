01 — Background

The Situation

During my internship at DigitizeBrand Hub, a Google Ads Search campaign called "Website traffic-Search-CNDT-24-11-2025" was live but essentially dead — 31 impressions, 26 clicks, zero conversions, and a ROAS of just 0.05. The campaign was spending budget but generating no measurable business results.

My task was to assist in diagnosing why the campaign was underperforming and support the team in restructuring it for real results.

The core problem: The campaign had extremely low impression share (only 31 impressions total), no conversion tracking properly set up, and likely poor keyword targeting and ad relevance — meaning Google wasn't showing the ads to enough of the right people, and even when it did, nothing was being measured.

My role as intern: I assisted in campaign auditing, keyword analysis, ad copy review, conversion tracking setup, and post-optimization monitoring. I worked under the team lead's supervision and learned hands-on how each change impacted performance in Google Ads.

03 — What We Did

Optimization Strategy — Step by Step

The campaign needed work across four areas: impression volume, keyword targeting, conversion tracking, and landing page alignment.

  • 1

    Campaign Audit — Why Were Impressions So Low?

    The first step was understanding why the campaign only got 31 impressions. We checked: keyword match types (likely too restrictive — exact match on low-volume terms), Quality Score, Ad Rank, and budget limits. Low impression share usually means Google isn't bidding the ads into the auction — either due to budget, keyword issues, or poor ad relevance.

  • 2

    Keyword Restructuring

    Reviewed keyword list and identified overly narrow or irrelevant terms that weren't triggering enough searches. Expanded to include broader and phrase match keywords with higher search volume. Added negative keywords to filter out irrelevant traffic and protect budget from wasted clicks.

  • 3

    Conversion Tracking Setup and Validation

    The campaign was recording 0 conversions partly because conversion tracking wasn't properly configured — no conversion actions were firing. We set up proper conversion tracking via Google Tag Manager, defined the conversion action (form submission / CTA click), and validated it was firing using GTM Preview and Google Ads conversion debugger.

  • 4

    Ad Copy Improvement

    Reviewed existing ad headlines and descriptions for relevance to keywords and landing page content. Rewrote headlines to include target keywords, a clear value proposition, and a strong CTA. Improved ad relevance directly improves Quality Score, which lowers CPC and raises Ad Rank — leading to more impressions at lower cost.

  • 5

    Budget Scaling After Validation

    Once conversions started coming in and the campaign showed positive signals, budget was scaled up. This allowed Google's algorithm to gather more data and optimize delivery more efficiently — reflected in the CPC dropping from ₹9.56 to ₹6.21 even as volume exploded.

  • 6

    Ongoing Monitoring and Reporting

    After launch, I monitored daily performance in Google Ads — watching CTR, CPC, conversion rate, and ROAS trends. Flagged anomalies to the team lead and helped document results in Excel for reporting purposes.

05 — Important Note on CTR

Why CTR Dropped and Why That's a Good Sign

At first glance, the CTR dropping from 83.87% to 22.95% looks like a problem. It isn't.

The original 83.87% CTR came from only 31 impressions — a tiny, likely accidental sample where almost everyone who saw the ad happened to click. This is statistically meaningless at such small volume.

After optimization, with 5,468 impressions, a 22.95% CTR is genuinely strong — industry benchmarks for search campaigns typically sit between 6–15%. Our 22.95% means the keywords and ad copy are well aligned with user intent.

Key insight: High CTR on low impressions = noise. High CTR on high impressions = signal. Always read metrics in context of scale.

06 — Key Learnings

What I Learned

🔍

Audit before acting

The instinct was to fix the ads. The real issue was impression share — a structural problem, not a creative one.

🎯

Conversion tracking is non-negotiable

Without proper conversion tracking, you can't optimize. The algorithm is blind without conversion signals.

📊

Quality Score drives efficiency

Better ad relevance → higher Quality Score → lower CPC. CPC dropped 35% not by bidding less, but by improving relevance.

⚖️

Read metrics in context

CTR "dropping" from 83% to 22% was actually great news. Small sample sizes produce misleading numbers.

📈

Scale what works, not what's comfortable

Budget scaling was held back out of caution. Once conversions validated performance, scaling delivered the ROI.

🔗

GTM + Google Ads = complete picture

Using GTM for conversion tracking gave precise event-level control that the native Google Ads tag alone wouldn't provide.
